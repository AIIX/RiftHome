Rift Home is a VR desktop for Linux on the Oculus Rift CV1: a Wayland compositor on RiftLinux's OpenXR runtime that
shows your apps as windows in a room around you, with a Home launcher, a dock, a Settings app and HDR video. Start it
from the login screen as its own session or from your desktop's app menu.

This is the first release.

## New

- **Your apps in the room.** Wayland apps, and X11 apps through XWayland, open as windows in front of you. Point at
  them with the Touch controllers, click, right-click and scroll; grab a window to move it, bring it nearer or push
  it away, resize, tilt or curve it, pin it or let it follow you. Window places are remembered.
- **Home and the dock.** A launcher with search and a card for every running app; a dock with the keyboard,
  notifications, screenshots, volume, the time and the network.
- **Cinema mode.** One window as a large screen in front of you, the room dimmed.
- **Recline.** For bed or sofa: hold a grip and press B (or the dock's Recline) and all your windows turn to where you
  look, above you when lying back; again, and they are upright.
- **HDR video.** Firefox and mpv play HDR10 and HLG video with its HDR description, shown in the headset as HDR;
  Stremio too, through its external player with mpv.
- **Rooms.** Three HDR panoramas of its own (Twilight lake, Mountain sunset, Night sky), your own 2:1 panoramas and
  photos (in `~/Pictures/RiftHome`), or your desktop's wallpapers.
- **A keyboard in the room** that opens under the text field you type into.
- **Notifications** from your apps, with their action buttons.
- **VR apps.** Switch between Rift Home and other VR apps that use RiftLinux's runtime; Chromium-based browsers get
  WebXR.
- **Games.** Steam and Proton games as windows; game mode turns the Touch controllers into a gamepad.
- **Settings in the headset:** Wi-Fi and wired network, Bluetooth, sound, date and time, the room and the windows'
  look, the controller buttons (a button, or two pressed together, for Home, the dock, clicks, the keyboard, a
  screenshot, recline, game mode, volume and more), power.
- **Lens adjustment.** A lens test as on the Rift's own setup, in Settings: a fine cross in each eye to fit the
  headset on your face and set the lens slider until the lines are sharp.
- **Your own login.** In its own session Rift Home unlocks your password store as your desktop does, so apps do not
  ask for a wallet password.

## Tested on

CachyOS with an NVIDIA graphics card (driver 615), an Oculus Rift CV1 with Touch controllers and sensors, RiftLinux
0.1.5. AMD and Intel graphics and other distributions are untested. The Fedora 44 and Ubuntu 26.10 packages are built
and install-tested in clean containers (the programs start, the files are in place), but have not yet been tried with a
headset: only CachyOS has.

## Install

After RiftLinux. Arch, CachyOS, EndeavourOS and Manjaro:

```sh
sudo pacman -U rift-home-0.1.0-1-x86_64.pkg.tar.zst
```

Fedora 44 and later:

```sh
sudo dnf install ./rift-home-0.1.0-1.fc44.x86_64.rpm
```

Ubuntu 26.10:

```sh
sudo apt install ./rift-home_0.1.0-1_amd64.deb
```

Other distributions build from source (wlroots 0.20, Qt 6.11 and wayland-protocols 1.49 are needed): see the README. Then log out
and choose "Rift Home" on the login screen, or start "Rift Home" from the app menu. The
[getting started guide](https://github.com/AIIX/RiftHome/blob/main/docs/getting-started.md) walks through the first
minutes, and [known limitations](https://github.com/AIIX/RiftHome/blob/main/docs/known-limitations.md) lists what
this release does not do yet.
