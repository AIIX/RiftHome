Rift Home is a VR desktop for Linux on the Oculus Rift CV1: a Wayland compositor on RiftLinux's OpenXR runtime that
shows your apps as windows in a room around you. This release is for films and for comfort: a 4K cinema screen in the
film's own shape, and windows that stay steady in the room.

## New

- **4K screens.** Maximised and full-screen windows and the cinema screen are 3840x2160 pixels at any apps' scale: a
  4K film plays at its own resolution, and a maximised app is drawn twice as finely. The resize button takes the
  cinema screen up to 7680x4320 (8K).
- **Films in their own shape.** A film in mpv goes into cinema in its own shape, with no black bars. A row under the
  screen switches between Original, Classic 4:3, IMAX film 1.43:1, TV 16:9, Widescreen 1.85:1, IMAX 1.90:1,
  Streaming 2:1 and Scope 2.39:1, all within the same frame, so switching back and forth keeps the screen's size.
- **A padlock for windows.** At the left end of the curve slider: a locked window stays where it is when you grab it,
  so a grip pressed by chance does not knock a film aside. The lock is remembered for the app.
- **A window's numbers.** Point at the resize button to see the window's pixels and shape, its colour (HDR or SDR)
  and depth, and how many of its pixels the headset gets.
- **Sharper at Picture quality 200 percent.** Windows are filtered to the picture the runtime draws, so a higher
  Picture quality shows more of their detail.
- **Controllers hide with their rays** when you hold still (Settings, "Hide when still"), so neither is in front of a
  film. Both come back when you move or press a button.
- **Settings for the best mpv picture** in [Apps](https://github.com/AIIX/RiftHome/blob/main/docs/apps.md#mpv).

## Fixed

- **Windows no longer seem to float or slide as you move your head.** A window could be pushed beyond the room's
  picture: it was drawn in front of the room while your eyes placed it behind it, and it seemed to slide with your
  head, which can make you feel sick. Windows now stay inside the room, with their controls.
- **Smooth window edges.** Window edges were hard and crawled as you moved your head; they now blend into the room.
  The controls around windows, the dock and the keyboard too.
- **Stremio's window can be resized** in cinema mode.

## Tested on

CachyOS with an NVIDIA graphics card, an Oculus Rift CV1 with Touch controllers and sensors, RiftLinux 0.1.5. WebXR
with Chromium 157 from Flathub. AMD and Intel graphics and other distributions
are untested. The Fedora 44 and Ubuntu 26.10 packages are built and install-tested in clean containers (the programs
start, the files are in place), but have not yet been tried with a headset: only CachyOS has.

## Install

After RiftLinux 0.1.5 or later. Arch, CachyOS, EndeavourOS and Manjaro:

```sh
sudo pacman -U rift-home-0.1.1-1-x86_64.pkg.tar.zst
```

Fedora 44 and later:

```sh
sudo dnf install ./rift-home-0.1.1-1.fc44.x86_64.rpm
```

Ubuntu 26.10:

```sh
sudo apt install ./rift-home_0.1.1-1_amd64.deb
```

The package installs over 0.1.0: your settings and window places stay. Other distributions build from source (wlroots
0.20, Qt 6.11 and wayland-protocols 1.49 are needed): see the README. The
[getting started guide](https://github.com/AIIX/RiftHome/blob/main/docs/getting-started.md) walks through the first
minutes, and [known limitations](https://github.com/AIIX/RiftHome/blob/main/docs/known-limitations.md) lists what
this release does not do yet.
